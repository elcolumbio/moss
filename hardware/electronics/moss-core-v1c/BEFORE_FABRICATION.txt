MOSS CORE V1C — AUDITED PROTOTYPE
15 October 2026 — KiCad 10.0.6
English publication corrected on 15 October 2026; electrical design unchanged.

This revision replaces V1B. Do not order V1B: a clean DRC did not validate USB
paths, decoupling returns or screw/cable clearances. AUDIT_V1B_V1C.html describes
the findings and their status. The audit is limited to files, consulted datasheets
and the R79 mechanical model; it does not guarantee the absence of every defect.

Core replaces the QT Py, its BFF, the INA board and servo signal adapter. No BFF
plugs into this PCB. The Cytron, audio amplifier, computer power supply and power
wiring remain external. The ESP32-S3 module is soldered directly onto Core.

REDESIGNED BOARD
60 × 65 × 1.6 mm PCB, R2 corners, four Ø2.7 non-plated holes for M2.5 screws.
Centres: (4,4), (56,4), (4,61), (56,61) mm; pitch 52 × 57 mm. Each screw has a
Ø8 reserve free of copper and components, including internal layers. Printed
supports must follow this interface. Old BFF mounts no longer dictate the outline.

150 components assembled on top; eight bottom test points and four mounting
holes are excluded from BOM/placement. Passives were enlarged to 0603 where
possible; only the two USB termination resistors remain 0402. Connector references
and RESET/BOOT are marked on top; complete connector functions are on the bottom
and in CONNECTOR_PINOUT.csv.

Signals are routed on F.Cu and B.Cu. In1.Cu and In2.Cu are GND-only planes with no
signal tracks. Direct capacitor-to-GND-via returns measure at most 1.1 mm in the
routing data. USB traces remain on F.Cu without signal vias, with terminations
near the module. The parallel trunk is symmetric; the reversible connector's
four-to-two pin fan-in stays local. Total branch copper lengths must not be
confused with propagation delay for each cable orientation.

POWER AND PROTECTION
USB-only input: immediate reverse-blocking diode, TPS2553 limiter, TLV62569 buck
and TLV803E supervisor. The 464k/100k divider at 0.1% is required: 3.384 V nominal,
static bounds 3.311–3.457 V under the assumptions in LOGIC_VALIDATION.json.
Static margin above the maximum reset-release threshold is about 122 mV; this
does not validate fast voltage dips.
Calculated hardware input-current limit: 387–491 mA. Design budget: 200 mA target,
350 mA continuous on the logic rail, radio disabled. The converter's 2 A rating is
not current available from USB. Firmware must respect pre-enumeration and suspend
current; the limiter alone does not implement that policy. Encoders and pull-ups
remain unpowered until USB configuration and must switch off in suspend.
Consumption, inrush, temperature and transient tests remain necessary.

The board supplies signals to Cytron and the servo bus; it does not carry their
power current. INA226 measures total pack current through an external 2 mohm
Kelvin shunt, to be selected with its dissipation and harness. At 15 A: 30 mV /
0.45 W; at 30 A: 60 mV / 1.8 W. Above approximately 40.96 A, the shunt-voltage
measurement range is exceeded with 2 mohm. Protect thin sense wires at the source.
No power shunt or battery fuse is included in the PCB assembly BOM.

INTERFACE REQUIREMENTS
J1: USB-C peripheral, Core data and power.
J2: PWM1 / DIR1 / PWM2 / DIR2 / GND to Cytron MDD10A. This order is Core-specific;
wire the harness to the correct labels on the Cytron.
J3/J4: GND / ENC_3V3 / A / B for left/right encoders. Power encoders from Core;
do not connect a separate 5 V supply to this harness. Buffers invert both phases,
which preserves their phase relationship.
J5: GND / servo TTL DATA. 3.3 V interface following the installed Waveshare (A)
adapter; arm power is external. No 12 V here.
J6: Kelvin+ / Kelvin− / GND / spare for a 3S pack. Measurement input, not board power.
J7: GND / BCLK / LRCLK / DIN / ENABLE / amplifier 5 V presence / spare.
MAX98357A keeps its own power supply. The 5 V rail no longer reaches a GPIO
through a resistor divider.
J8: current-limited 3.3 V source / normally-closed dry-contact return.
J9: GND / logic permission / active-low fault. Its pull-up does not detect an
unplugged optional fault input. Permission follows master actuator arming;
firmware must obtain it before requesting motor movement if it drives a common
cutoff. This connector is not, by itself, a power stop.

Hardware masking blocks PWM and servo transmission on a fault. The MCU reads
both latches. A hardware fault must require explicit rearming; do not reuse the
existing client's ERR not_armed response, which triggers automatic rearming.
DC cutoff contacts, BMS, fuses and braking behavior remain part of a separately
reviewed power harness.

SOFTWARE STILL TO PORT
Current QT Py firmware cannot be flashed unchanged: pin assignments, supervision,
INA226 and USB differ. Files under firmware/ define a contract, not executable
firmware.
Selected profile: one CDC ACM interface multiplexing motors/servos plus UAC audio
to I2S. A host broker exposes two pseudo-TTYs to existing clients. Two standard
CDCs plus UAC would exceed the ESP32-S3 endpoint budget. ALSA/TTS remains a
functional objective, but this profile and broker must be implemented and tested.
I2S presets alone do not replace the rover's audio.
GPIO44 and GPIO1 read the hardware latches. GPIO42 controls encoder power;
GPIO2 reads the USB input fault. USB ROM and BOOT/RESET remain the programming
method; TX0 is available at a test point, but RX0 is not.

FABRICATION
Four layers, 35 um external copper, 15.2 um internal planes; target stackup
JLC04161H-3313, nominal F.Cu/In1.Cu dielectric 99.4 um, Er 4.1. The stackup
recorded in the project is nominal, not a manufacturer's commitment.
Differential USB target 90 ohm +/-10%, trunk W/S=0.15/0.15 mm. The independent
masked model estimates about 88–90 ohm; combined variations can exceed the range.
Request impedance control and actual tolerances from the manufacturer before
ordering. Do not silently substitute a different stackup.
General rules: minimum trace/clearance 0.15 mm, copper-to-edge 0.30 mm, ordinary
via diameter/drill 0.60/0.30 mm. The ESP32 footprint's 0.20 mm thermal-pad via
drills are an official-package exception; confirm paste/tenting strategy with
the assembler. No blind microvias.
A local rule allows 0.15 mm copper-to-hole clearance only between J1 elements,
to respect the official GCT USB4105 locating-pin geometry. The general rule
remains 0.25 mm. Screw copper constraints are not relaxed.
Gerbers, separate PTH/NPTH drills, grouped BOM, positions and assembly PDFs are
supplied for review/prototyping. Positions use absolute KiCad coordinates in mm,
TOP side; verify rotations with the assembler. No production quote, supplier
stock or total cost has been confirmed.

MECHANICS
mechanical/ supplies the dimensioned interface, DXF and revised placement proposal.
PCB origin in native R79 coordinates: (45,−80,74) mm. Four spacers suspended under
the arm platform are proposed. Attachments, inserts/nuts, screw lengths and access
remain to design in native CAD; no hull changes were applied. The old BFF location
was abandoned because plugs collided with the hull.
The board overhangs the battery placeholder by 16 mm, with nominal 5.5 mm clearance
under PCB, or 2.5 mm if 3 mm is reserved for a head/nut. Nine plug envelopes and
their upward routing corridors pass surface checks with an extra 0.5 mm margin.
These are service-space assumptions, not measurements of the real harness.
Point-containment and triangle tests do not replace native-solid calculations;
the cover mesh has two non-manifold edges. Verify the real battery, removal,
plugs, cover, screw access and vibration before volume manufacture.
The STEP/STL carrier in mechanical/bench_only/ is for bench use only. It would
interfere with the battery in the proposed position: do not use it as an installed
rover support. Read mechanical/FINAL_MECHANICAL_INTEGRATION_REPORT.txt.
Simplified ESP32 and watchdog 3D envelopes are identified in models/ENVELOPE_MODELS.json;
other available models come from the KiCad library.

issues. Native reports record rules and checks disabled by default; no wiring-error

486 pins (472 connected, 14 NC) compared with native XML; 16,384 logic combinations,
